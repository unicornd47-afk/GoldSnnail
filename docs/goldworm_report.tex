\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We present GoldWorm, a 0.92\,MB spiking neural network (SNN) operating in hyperbolic (Poincaré-ball) space with 72\,$\mu$s inference latency. Originally conceived as an ARC-AGI reasoning candidate, GoldWorm instead delivers three empirically grounded contributions: (1) a multi-modal SNN achieving 83.3\,\% semantic relevance across DVS event streams and digit grids; (2) a hyperbolic embedding space that separates ARC tasks with an inter/intra ratio of 3.66, but \emph{systematically fails} to encode compositional transformations as vectors (Silhouette 0.189) or to support nearest-neighbor retrieval (0\,\% exact match); and (3) a continual-learning substrate where catastrophic forgetting reaches 98.7\,\% without replay, validating the necessity of experience replay in hyperbolic SNNs. Our work demonstrates that hyperbolic geometry captures task \emph{identity} but not task \emph{mechanism}---a negative result with significant implications for neural-symbolic ARC solvers. All code, benchmarks, and failed hypotheses are documented for reproducibility.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The ARC Prize \cite{arc_prize} frames a stark challenge: solve 400 novel grid-based reasoning tasks with human-like few-shot generalization. Large language models (LLMs) such as o3 achieve $\sim$80\,\% accuracy at \$200 per task \cite{o3_arc}; we asked whether a \emph{physically efficient} substrate---a spiking neural network (SNN) in hyperbolic space---could compete not on raw accuracy, but on \emph{efficiency} (accuracy per dollar).

GoldWorm was architected around three hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{H1 (Efficiency)}: SNNs with hyperbolic embeddings can match multi-modal tasks at $<$1\,MB model size and $<$100\,$\mu$s latency.
    \item \textbf{H2 (Reasoning)}: Compositional ARC transformations (rotation, color mapping, object counting) are encoded as \emph{consistent vectors} in the Poincaré ball, enabling vector-arithmetic reasoning.
    \item \textbf{H3 (Continuity)}: Hyperbolic criticality ($\tau = -1.92$) provides inherent protection against catastrophic forgetting.
\end{enumerate}

This report documents the \emph{verification} of H1, the \emph{refutation} of H2, and the \emph{partial refutation} of H3. Section \ref{sec:discussion} synthesizes these findings into a revised research agenda.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hyperbolic Neural Networks.} Nickel \& Kiela \cite{ nickel2017poincare} demonstrated that hierarchical data embeds more efficiently in hyperbolic than Euclidean space. We extend this to \emph{spiking} activations and grid-structured vision tasks.

\paragraph{ARC Solvers.} State-of-the-art ARC solvers (Arborist \cite{arborist}, AlphaDot \cite{alphadot}) rely on domain-specific language (DSL) search. We hypothesized that a learned hyperbolic embedding could \emph{replace} explicit program search; our results show it cannot, but may \emph{complement} it.

\paragraph{Continual Learning in SNNs.} Replay-based methods dominate \cite{replay_snn}. We test whether hyperbolic geometry alone (without replay) mitigates forgetting.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Architecture}

GoldWorm consists of three Rust modules:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{src/snn/}: Leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons with sparse connectivity.
    \item \texttt{src/semantics/}: Poincaré-ball embeddings ($\mathbb{D}^{16}$, target radius $r=0.75$).
    \item \texttt{src/vision/}: Grid encoder (100D features $\rightarrow$ 32D hidden $\rightarrow$ 16D hyperbolic point).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Feature Engineering for ARC Grids}

Raw 10$\times$10 pixel values proved insufficient (Section \ref{sec:arc_negative}). We engineered a 100D feature vector comprising:
\begin{itemize}
    \item[0--9] Color histogram (10D)
    \item[10--19] Row averages (10D)
    \item[20--29] Column averages (10D)
    \item[30--54] 5$\times$5 center patch (25D)
    \item[55--74] Border statistics (20D)
    \item[75--99] Symmetry features (25D)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training Protocols}

\paragraph{Multi-Modal (N-MNIST).} DVS event streams + static digit grids, 3-digit semantic relevance task.
\paragraph{Continual Learning.} Sequential 10-digit N-MNIST, with and without experience replay.
\paragraph{ARC-AGI-1.} Self-supervised distance minimization between input/output grid pairs on 400 training tasks.

\subsection{Feature Engineering: Task Separability}

The engineered feature vector raised the ARC inter/intra-task ratio from 1.28 (raw pixels) to 3.66 (features), crossing the usability threshold of 1.5 and approaching excellent separability ($>$3.0).

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/fig_feature_engineering.png}
    \caption{Impact of feature engineering on ARC task separability in hyperbolic space. Raw pixels produce overlapping tasks (ratio 1.28); engineered features yield clear separation (ratio 3.66).}
    \label{fig:features}
\end{figure}

This confirms that the \emph{Poincaré ball is a suitable container} for ARC task semantics---provided the input representation captures relational structure (histograms, symmetry, row/column statistics) rather than raw pixel positions.

\section{Results: Refuted Hypotheses}
\label{sec:arc_negative}

\subsection{H2: Transformation Vectors Do Not Cluster}

We computed 2,676 transformation vectors (output $\ominus$ input in tangent space) across 811 ARC tasks and applied k-means clustering ($k \in [2,20]$). The optimal Silhouette score was 0.189 at $k=2$---far below the 0.5 threshold required for usable clustering.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{figures/fig_arc_clustering.png}
    \caption{Silhouette analysis of transformation vectors. The observed score (0.189) indicates that ARC transformations are not encoded as consistent, reusable vectors in hyperbolic space.}
    \label{fig:silhouette}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Interpretation.} ARC tasks are \emph{compositions}, not atoms. A task may combine ``rotate 90$^\circ$ + color map + fill border.'' The resulting transformation vector is dominated by the \emph{composition}, not by any individual operation. Therefore, two tasks sharing ``rotate 90$^\circ$'' do \emph{not} produce similar vectors. The hyperbolic space encodes \textbf{task identity}, not \textbf{task mechanism}.

\subsection{H2 (continued): Retrieval Fails at Zero Percent}

Nearest-neighbor retrieval in the hyperbolic space achieved 0\,\% exact-match accuracy on 100 held-out ARC tasks. Critically, the average hyperbolic distance between retrieved and true outputs was 0.0598---deceptively small---yet the Hamming distance averaged 341.55 wrong pixels (on 10$\times$10 grids), with 90\,\% size mismatches.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_retrieval_error.png}
    \caption{Retrieval failure modes. Embedding proximity (0.0598) does not imply structural or pixel-wise similarity. The hyperbolic neighborhood is semantically coherent at the \emph{task} level, but not at the \emph{solution} level.}
    \label{fig:retrieval}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Implication.} Task similarity in embedding space \emph{does not imply solution transferability}. This refutes the core assumption of our Phase-2 roadmap and forces a pivot from vector-arithmetic reasoning to either (a) explicit program search or (b) hybrid retrieval-synthesis approaches.

\subsection{H3: Hyperbolic Geometry Does Not Prevent Forgetting}

Without experience replay, catastrophic forgetting on sequential N-MNIST digits reached 98.7\,\%. Average accuracy collapsed from 80.2\,\% (with replay) to 38.9\,\% (without replay).

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/fig_continual_learning.png}
    \caption{Continual learning with and without replay. Hyperbolic criticality ($\tau=-1.92$) provides \emph{no measurable protection} against catastrophic forgetting in the absence of replay.}
    \label{fig:continual}
\end{figure}

This is a valuable negative result: it falsifies the hypothesis that hyperbolic geometry alone mitigates forgetting, and establishes replay as a \emph{necessary} component in efficient SNN continual learning.

\section{The Cross-Modal Bugfix: A Case Study}
\label{sec:bugfix}

A wiring error in the cross-modal projection layer initially produced 3.8\,\% semantic relevance. Correcting the connectivity (single-line fix in \texttt{src/snn/mod.rs}) raised performance to 83.3\,\%. We document this explicitly because it illustrates a broader principle: hyperbolic spaces are \emph{sensitive to connectivity structure}. A miswired projection collapses multi-modal alignment; a correct projection enables robust semantic binding.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the Hyperbolic Space Actually Learns}

Our three ARC findings (Ratio 3.66, Silhouette 0.189, Retrieval 0\,\%) resolve into a consistent picture:

\begin{quote}
\textit{The Poincaré ball learns to cluster task \emph{identities}---it knows that Task A is different from Task B---but it does not learn to factorize tasks into reusable \emph{mechanisms}. Compositional transformations (rotation $\circ$ color-map $\circ$ fill) produce monolithic vectors that are not decomposable.}
\end{quote}

This is not a failure of hyperbolic geometry; it is a \emph{fundamental limitation of pure embedding-based reasoning} on compositional tasks. It aligns with recent critiques of ``geometry-as-reasoning'' approaches \cite{geometry_critique}.

\subsection{Implications for ARC Solvers}

A viable GoldWorm-based ARC solver must be \textbf{hybrid}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hyperbolic router:} Use the embedding space to identify the task \emph{family} (ratio 3.66 proves this works).
    \item \textbf{Explicit program search:} Use a small DSL (rotate, flip, fill, count) to solve the task within the identified family.
    \item \textbf{Efficiency wrapper:} The router runs in 72\,$\mu$s; only promising program candidates are evaluated.
\end{enumerate}

This converts GoldWorm from a ``reasoning engine'' into a ``reasoning accelerator.''

\subsection{ARC-AGI-2: The Interactive Pivot}

ARC-AGI-2 introduces feedback loops: the agent may ask questions, request examples, or execute actions. Our SNN's 72\,$\mu$s latency and online continual-learning capacity (with replay) may offer genuine advantages over static LLMs in this setting. We leave this as primary future work.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hybrid ARC Solver:} Implement the router+DSL architecture described in Section \ref{sec:discussion} and evaluate on the full 400-task evaluation set.
    \item \textbf{ARC-AGI-2 Evaluation:} Test whether interactive feedback loops exploit GoldWorm's latency and online-learning advantages.
    \item \textbf{No-Replay Alternatives:} Investigate orthogonal replay (generative replay in hyperbolic space) and modularity (task-specific sub-manifolds) as alternatives to raw experience replay.
    \item \textbf{Efficiency Leaderboard Submission:} Submit a minimal GoldWorm entry to establish a cost-per-task baseline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

GoldWorm began as an attempt to solve ARC-AGI through hyperbolic vector reasoning. It ends Phase 1 as something arguably more valuable: a \textbf{rigorously bounded} efficient SNN whose capabilities and limitations are empirically established.

We have shown that:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A 0.92\,MB SNN can achieve 83.3\,\% multi-modal semantic relevance and 80.2\,\% continual-learning accuracy.
    \item Hyperbolic spaces separate ARC tasks (ratio 3.66) but \emph{cannot} encode their compositional transformations (Silhouette 0.189).
    \item Nearest-neighbor retrieval in embedding space achieves 0\,\% exact match, proving that task similarity $\neq$ solution transferability.
    \item Catastrophic forgetting reaches 98.7\,\% without replay, falsifying the hypothesis that geometry alone protects memory.
\end{itemize}

These negative results are not setbacks; they are \textbf{guardrails}. They prevent us from investing 15 months in a vector-reasoning pipeline that cannot work, and redirect us toward hybrid architectures where GoldWorm's true strengths---efficiency, latency, and multi-modal binding---can be exploited.

The ARC Prize has two leaderboards. We may never top the accuracy board. But with documented efficiency, reproducible benchmarks, and honest failure analysis, we can top the \emph{science} board.
